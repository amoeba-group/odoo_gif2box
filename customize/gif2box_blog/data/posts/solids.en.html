A baby's first solid meal is a milestone, and having a few tools ready makes it much less daunting. This guide lists what you need, how to choose materials, and a few tips to keep everything clean.

When do babies start solids?

The World Health Organization (WHO) recommends exclusive breastfeeding for the first six months, then introducing solid foods. Signs that a baby is ready include sitting with support, holding the head steady and showing interest when others eat. Every baby is different, so check with your paediatrician before you start.

The basic starting-solids checklist

  • A suction bowl or plate: keeps food in place on the table.
  • Soft silicone spoons: gentle on gums and mouth.
  • A bib: silicone ones with a catch pocket are easier to wash than cloth.
  • A masher and a strainer: for smooth textures in the first weeks.
  • A lidded freezer tray: cook once, serve several meals.
  • Food containers: for meals on the go.
  • Cooking tools just for baby food: heat-resistant silicone spatulas, a small pan.

Choosing safe materials

Platinum silicone

Soft, heat-resistant, BPA-free, slow to hold odours and easy to clean. Ideal for spoons, bowls, bibs and freezer trays.

304 stainless steel

Durable and rust-resistant, suited to mashers, strainers and pans.

PPSU

Light, hard to break and heat-resistant, often used for bottles and training cups.

Avoid plastics of unknown origin, especially for anything used to cook or warm food.

Cleaning and storage tips

  • Wash tools right after the meal, before food dries on.
  • Clean and sterilise each item as its manufacturer recommends.
  • Let everything dry fully on a rack before putting it away.
  • Date your freezer portions so you use them in order.
